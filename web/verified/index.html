Verification & affiliation

The marks on yappy, and what they actually mean

A mark next to a name is a claim yappy itself is making — about who somebody is, or where they belong. Because it is our claim and not the account's, nobody can give themselves one. This page is the whole system, written down.

One rule carries everything

Circles are people. Squircles are places. Every avatar, every badge, every mark in the app follows this shape rule, so you can read a screen at a glance: a round mark is about a person, a rounded-square mark is about a group.

There are a few more (beta testers and bot developers carry their own small marks), but these three are the ones that assert identity. Personal badges have no application form — staff grant them, every grant is recorded, and a badge can be taken back the same way it was given.

Verified groups

A group badge says this is the real one — the actual community, not a copy wearing its name and logo. It shows on the group card in every member's home screen, on invite links wherever they are shared, and on the group's page.

✓ the garden ✦ partner community

Groups can hold verified, partner, or staff. One badge, not a collection — a group is one thing.

How a group gets verified

  1. Open your group's settings. You need to be the owner or an admin — the request speaks for the group, so it sits with the people who do.
  2. Tap Request verification. Three questions, one at a time: what the group is about, where else it lives (a site, a Discord, an Instagram — optional, but it helps), and why it should be verified.
  3. Send it. The request goes straight in front of yappy staff. One open request per group — sending twice will just tell you it is already in the queue.
  4. That's it. If the answer is yes, the badge simply appears on the group. Nothing else to do, nothing to install, nothing to pay.

What staff actually look at: is this group real, active, and the original? Member count matters less than you would think. A small group that is clearly the genuine home of its community beats a large one that copied a name.

Affiliate badges

An affiliate badge is a group lending its credibility to a person: the group's own logo, as a small squircle, riding next to that person's name in every chat — the way a jersey says which team somebody plays for.

maya G

Read it as: this person speaks for that group, and the group itself said so.

Three rules keep it honest:

1
Only verified groups can affiliate
Credibility has to exist before it can be lent. An unverified group has no affiliate option at all.
2
Only owners and admins can grant it
From the member list on the group's page — mark or remove an affiliate in two taps. Revoking always works, even if the group later loses its badge.
3
Wearing it is the person's choice
Being affiliated puts nothing on your name by itself. You pick which affiliation to display in your own settings — or none. A group can vouch for you; it cannot dress you.

What no mark ever means

A badge confirms identity, not behaviour. It is not an endorsement of what anyone says, and it does not exempt anyone from the rules — a verified account that breaks them loses the badge along with everything else.

Seen someone impersonating a person or a group? Report them in the app, or at yappy.gg/bug. Impersonation reports get read first.
